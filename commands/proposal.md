## Inputs
Use the RFP or buyer request, desired outcomes, budget/currency, timeframe, deliverables, acceptance criteria, available staff, effort/rates, approved credentials and known dependencies. Reuse confirmed company context. Keep unknowns explicit; do not invent client references, capacity, discounts or tax treatment.

## Analysis
1. Build a requirement matrix with stable IDs: requirement, response/deliverable, acceptance evidence, owner and status. Preserve mandatory language, security and submission requirements. Clarify ambiguous requirements that materially change scope; otherwise put the exact assumption in the matrix and mark that requirement provisional. For example, "weekly reports" does not establish whether project updates or operational finance reports are required; do not silently substitute one for the other.
2. Define phases, outputs, exclusions, client dependencies and review rounds. Match required expertise/languages to confirmed capacity and realistic dates. An unavailable capability is a gap, not a promise.
3. Reconcile hours × rates, expenses, contingency, discounts and taxes separately. Show estimated effort value versus proposed fee and budget; do not imply tax-inclusive affordability when tax treatment is unknown. Label estimates and unapproved commercial terms.
4. Offer feasible scope/fee options when constraints conflict. Preserve mandatory requirements; do not quietly remove them or assume an authorized discount. Identify the decision needed before a firm offer can be issued.

## Deliverable and acceptance
Return a proposal with needs, supported differentiators, approach, deliverables, team/capacity, timeline, fee options and an acceptance/independence note. Include the requirement matrix, fee calculation and open decisions as editable appendices.

Complete when each requirement is met, explicitly conditional or unmet; fees reconcile; and resources and commitments are supported. Report technical soundness and request fulfillment separately. An unresolved mandatory requirement or unaffordable fee blocks an executable offer, even if the draft is polished. Do not send or accept the proposal on the client's behalf.
